Checkout is where intent becomes revenue, or doesn’t. Stores pour money into traffic and product pages, then leave checkout alone. Small changes here often move completed orders more than another ad.

## Measure the drop

What is add-to-cart, versus checkout started, versus order completed? If those events are not in GA4 or Shopify Analytics, set them up. The gap between checkout started and order completed is abandonment. The industry average is high, **60–80%**. Improving it by **5–10%** is a real revenue lift.

## Fewer fields

Every extra field increases drop-off. Autocomplete city and PIN. Ask only for what you need. Pre-fill email and shipping from the cart when you can.

## Guest checkout

Forcing an account before payment kills conversions. Let people check out as guests, and offer “create an account after the order” in one click. An account should be a benefit, not a gate.

## Trust, shipping, and payment

Security badges, “secure checkout,” and a clear return policy near the pay button reduce anxiety. Shopify’s native checkout is a solid base. Custom work should add clarity, not clutter.

Show shipping cost, or free shipping, early. If there is a threshold, say so in the cart and again at checkout. Show the payment methods people actually use, including UPI or buy-now-pay-later when that is the audience.

## Mobile, email, speed

Buttons should be thumb-sized. One page often beats a long wizard on a phone. A recovery sequence — one hour, 24 hours, 72 hours — can bring back **5–15%** of abandoned checkouts. Lead with what they wanted. A discount is optional, not the whole email.

Slow or broken checkout is a silent killer. Stay on Shopify checkout, or a proven headless path, and test the payment flow on a phone.

Treat checkout as a funnel. Track completion by device and by source. If you are not doing this, you are leaving money in the cart — which is the second half of [D2C performance](/#dtc), after the ad and the product page.
