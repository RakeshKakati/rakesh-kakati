Founders often ask: “What’s a good conversion rate for a Shopify store?” The honest answer is: it depends on your vertical, traffic source, and device mix. Benchmarks give you a target and a way to spot leaks. Here is a data-backed snapshot for 2026.

Across ecommerce, the average Shopify store converts between **2.5% and 3.5%**. Top quartile stores sit at **4–5%** or higher. If you’re under 2%, you’re leaving money on the table — and the fix is usually not more traffic. It is conversion rate optimization.

## By vertical

D2C fashion and accessories often land in the **2.5–3.5%** range. Beauty and supplements can go higher (**3–4%**) because of repeat buyers and stronger intent. Home and electronics tend to be lower (**1.5–2.5%**) because the purchase takes longer to consider. Niche brands with a clear audience often outperform broad stores by a full point or more.

## By source

Paid traffic converts lower than organic. Meta and Google ads might sit at **1.5–2.5%** on average. Email and retargeting can be **4–6%**. A traffic audit is not enough. Segment conversion rate by source, and work the highest-intent channels first.

## By device

Mobile converts at roughly half of desktop in many categories. If mobile conversion is under **1.5%**, you have a mobile problem, not a “people don’t buy on mobile” problem. Checkout friction, slow load, and an unclear call to action are the usual causes.

The lever is not hitting the average. It is moving from the bottom quartile to the top. That usually means better product pages, a smoother checkout, deliberate upsells, and retention flows. Treat the benchmark as the start of a roadmap, not a grade.

If the store is under these numbers, the work sits in [D2C performance](/#dtc): the page, the checkout, and the loop that brings the second order.
