Selling outcomes, not tasks, needs a repeatable system. This is the five-step frame I use with D2C stores: traffic, funnel, experiments, order value, and retention.

## 1. Traffic audit

Before touching the product page, know where people come from and what they do. Split organic, paid, email, and social. Often **20% of traffic drives 80% of revenue**. Put more into that slice. If mobile is 60% of traffic and 20% of revenue, you have a mobile problem. That decides where to start.

## 2. Funnel

Map landing to purchase: homepage, collection, product, cart, checkout. Plot conversion at each step. The biggest drop is usually product to cart, or cart to checkout. That tells you whether to fix the page and the proof, or the checkout and the friction.

## 3. Experiments

Test one change at a time: headline, button, proof, images, layout. Keep a test running, one in design, and one in review. This is how conversion moves from **1.5% toward 2.5%** and past it. Write down what worked.

## 4. Average order value

Same traffic, more revenue per order. Bundles, upsells, and a free-shipping threshold. Many stores can add **20–40%** to AOV with a structured offer instead of a blanket discount.

## 5. Retention

The first purchase is the start. Welcome, post-purchase, and win-back. SMS for delivery when it fits. Subscriptions for things people buy again. Repeat customers have a higher lifetime value and a lower cost to acquire. Measure repeat rate by cohort, and spend the first 30 to 90 days getting them back.

Together this is a revenue engine, not “a bit of CRO” and “a bit of email.” Document the process and attach numbers from the work. That is how the right founders — the ones who pay for outcomes — find you.
