Most Shopify stores do not have a traffic problem. They have a conversion and order-value problem. The same visitors could be worth **40–60%** more. That leak is real money.

## Product pages that don’t convert

People land, skim, and leave. The fix is a clearer offer, proof near the button, one dominant action, a fast load, and a layout that works on a phone. Many stores see a **20–30%** lift in add-to-cart from that alone. If product-to-cart is under **10%**, start here.

## Cart and checkout

People add, then bail. Unexpected shipping, a forced account, too many fields, or a slow checkout. Guest checkout, shipping shown early, fewer fields, and trust next to the pay button. Recovery email can bring back **5–15%** of lost orders. A **10%** better completion rate is a large jump in revenue.

## Order value left on the table

No bundle, no upsell, no shipping threshold usually means a missed **15–25%** on average order value. “Frequently bought together,” a post-add offer, and “add ₹X for free shipping” are the usual tests.

## One-and-done customers

Without a post-purchase and win-back flow, the second order never gets asked for. The first purchase is the expensive one. A welcome series, a review request, and a “we miss you” note are lifetime value, not fluff.

## Traffic that doesn’t match the page

Paid traffic to a generic homepage converts poorly. Match the landing page and the offer to the ad and the audience. Sometimes cutting low-intent traffic beats buying more of it.

Stop asking for more visitors. Ask for more revenue per visitor. Audit traffic, the funnel, the product page, checkout, order value, and retention. Fix the biggest leak first. Most stores can close a large part of that **40–60%** gap in a few months.

That is the job of [D2C performance](/#dtc): not more ads, more revenue from what you already have.
